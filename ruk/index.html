When a pause is not enough

Friction you can dial up, one rule at a time.

Daily limits

Screen time limits per app or across a group, with a warning before you run out. Then block, or ask again harder.

Schedules

A window where an app simply does not open — including one that crosses midnight. Set it once, decide nothing in the moment.

Focus sessions

Start from the app, a Quick Settings tile or a home screen widget, with an allow list for what you actually need.

Feed blocking Experimental

Stops Reels, Shorts, Explore, Stories and a home feed without closing the app they live in, so your messages and search still work. Six feeds, each tested on a real phone — and if an app update stops one being recognised, Ruk says so instead of pretending.

Strict mode

For the days you cannot be trusted with your own settings. Weakening a rule can be made to wait — it can never be made impossible.

Attention budget

A daily allowance for the whole habit, not one app. Days under budget build a streak that forgives one bad day for every seven good ones.

What it tells you afterwards

Your real screen time, from Android’s own figures — not a guess, and not a leaderboard.

  • Screen time per app and per hour of the day.
  • How many opens you abandoned, and roughly what that saved you.
  • Which app you always open next, once the first one has you.
  • Days under budget, and a streak with a little forgiveness built in.
  • Export the lot to a file you choose, any time.

Questions

Does Ruk need Accessibility access?

The pause does, because that is what lets Ruk notice an app opening and get there first. If you would rather not grant it, Ruk still works in observe-only mode: it reads Android’s own usage figures and shows you the insights, without interrupting anything.

Can I get out of a block?

Always. Every restriction has a documented way out and a screen inside the app lists them all. Strict mode can make weakening a setting wait — it can never make it impossible. Ruk never uses device admin, never hides itself, and can always be uninstalled.

What does feed blocking actually block?

Six feed surfaces — Reels, Explore and Stories, Shorts, a home feed and a Stories tray — each one tested on a real phone. It leaves the app itself open, so messages and search still work. It is labelled experimental in the app because a feed is somebody else’s screen and they change it whenever they like; when Ruk stops recognising one, it tells you instead of pretending.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. Ruk opens no connection of its own and sends nothing. There is no server, no analytics and no crash reporting, and everything it records lives in this phone’s app-private storage.

Is Pro a subscription?

No — one payment through Google Play, once. Anything you configure before buying is kept, and unlocking turns it on with nothing to set up again.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not today. Ruk is Android only.
